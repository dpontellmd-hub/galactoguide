# Trusted Essentials

> Clinician-reviewed help choosing practical feeding and pumping products without turning GalactoGuide into a storefront.

## Context / Why

Families already spend money on feeding and pumping products, often with little guidance about what is useful, optional, compatible, or unnecessary. GalactoGuide can make those decisions easier, but a conventional shop would blur the line between independent clinical guidance and commercial incentives.

The opportunity is to monetize useful curation while making the conflict visible and keeping it structurally unable to influence clinical content.

## Product Definition

**Recommended name:** Trusted Essentials

- Organize around situations and decisions, not brands or a product catalog.
- Explain what an item does, who may benefit, who probably does not need it, and how to choose one.
- Recommend product criteria first; show two or more reasonable examples second.
- Keep every clinical rating, substance entry, and search result independent of commercial relationships.
- Treat purchase links as optional convenience, never as the clinical answer.

## Goals & Non-Goals

### Goals

- Reduce confusing or unnecessary purchases.
- Preserve GalactoGuide's evidence-first credibility.
- Create a modest, transparent affiliate-revenue path.
- Give families useful lower-cost and no-purchase alternatives.
- Make commercial influence easy to understand at the moment of recommendation.

### Non-goals

- Become a general baby-products store.
- Sell or monetize supplements, galactagogues, or products making supply-treatment claims.
- Accept paid ranking, sponsored inclusion, or brand-written clinical copy.
- Label products as “best,” “doctor approved,” or “must-have.”
- Put purchase prompts inside substance evidence or safety sections.

## Audience / Users

- Parents and caregivers deciding what equipment may help in a specific feeding situation.
- Clinicians who want a concise, shareable explanation of product-selection criteria.

## Editorial Trust Charter

- Inclusion is based on a written clinical and usability rubric before revenue is considered.
- Compensation never changes inclusion, order, evidence language, or review outcome.
- Each recommendation names its reviewer and last review date.
- Each commercial link says plainly that GalactoGuide may earn a commission.
- Equivalent products and a non-affiliate or generic alternative are shown when available.
- Brands cannot buy placement or approve editorial copy.
- Sponsored content, if ever introduced, is visually separate and never presented as clinical guidance.
- Core evidence and safety content remains free and contains no commerce-driven ranking.

## Core Features (MVP)

### 1. Decision-led collections

Start with three narrow collections where product choice is practical rather than medicinal:

- Pumping away from home
- Finding the right flange fit
- Cleaning, storing, and transporting milk

Each collection answers:

- What problem is this solving?
- Do I need to buy anything?
- What features matter?
- What can I skip?
- What are reasonable budget, standard, and premium examples?

### 2. Product guidance cards

Each card includes:

- neutral item/category name;
- “may help when” and “probably unnecessary when” guidance;
- selection criteria and compatibility notes;
- known limitations or safety considerations;
- clinician reviewer and review date;
- two or more examples when practical;
- price band rather than a brittle exact price;
- clearly labeled outbound purchase link.

### 3. Disclosure at the decision point

Place this directly above or beside every paid link:

> If you buy through this link, GalactoGuide may earn a commission. That does not affect what we include or how we review it.

Also provide a short “How recommendations are chosen” page linked from every collection.

### 4. No-purchase path

Every collection should be able to say one of the following honestly:

- You may already have what you need.
- Ask your pump manufacturer, insurer, hospital, or lactation clinic before buying.
- This is optional convenience, not a clinical necessity.

### 5. Lightweight validation

- Track collection views and outbound clicks without sending health situations or substance names to retailers.
- Ask one optional question: “Did this help you decide what you actually needed?”
- Do not optimize clinical copy for conversion rate.

## Placement in GalactoGuide

- Add **Trusted Essentials** as a secondary destination beside **Trusted resources**.
- Do not add a bottom tab or name it **Shop** in the MVP.
- Keep it out of substance evidence, efficacy, and safety cards.
- Later, add restrained contextual links only from non-medicinal educational content, such as flange-fit guidance.

## Monetization Model

### Recommended first model: disclosed affiliate links

- Lowest operational burden and easiest to reverse.
- No inventory, fulfillment, returns, or product-liability expansion.
- Revenue is a byproduct of useful curation, not the basis of inclusion.

### Later option: paid membership

- Monetize saved plans, checklists, and clinician-authored education rather than product preference.
- This may align incentives better than increasing affiliate conversion.

### Avoid

- Directly selling health-related products under the physician's brand in the first release.
- Exclusive brand arrangements.
- Paid placement or “featured product” fees.
- Monetized supplements or supply-boosting claims.

## Open Questions & Risks

- **Business priority:** Is the goal near-term affiliate revenue, a trust-building service that supports Dyad visits, or eventual subscription value?
- **Reviewer identity:** Will Dr. Pontell personally review every product category and update?
- **Retailers:** Can each category offer multiple merchants or a non-affiliate option?
- **Maintenance:** Who owns recalls, broken links, compatibility changes, and periodic re-review?
- **Privacy:** Affiliate and analytics links must not expose sensitive profile, situation, or browsing data.
- **Trust measurement:** Click revenue alone cannot determine success; perceived independence must be measured directly.

## Phased Roadmap

- **Phase 1 — Trust test:** Publish three non-transactional collections with selection criteria, no-purchase guidance, and a short trust survey.
- **Phase 2 — Transparent links:** Add clearly disclosed affiliate links to the same editorially selected examples; compare trust feedback before and after.
- **Phase 3 — Expand carefully:** Add only categories that pass the rubric, plus change logs, re-review dates, and recall/link monitoring.
- **Phase 4 — Consider commerce:** Evaluate a direct store or curated kits only if users strongly value consolidation and the ethical, legal, fulfillment, and liability model is sound.

## Success Criteria

- Users can explain why an item was included without mentioning commission.
- Users notice and understand the financial disclosure.
- Trust feedback does not materially decline after paid links are introduced.
- Collections help users avoid unnecessary purchases as well as choose useful ones.
- No brand payment changes product inclusion or order.
- No sensitive health context is shared with merchants or affiliate networks.
- Revenue per collection is measured alongside helpfulness, trust, and maintenance burden.

## TLDR

- Build **Trusted Essentials**, not a **Shop**.
- Lead with what to buy, what to skip, and how to choose; brands come second.
- Start with non-medicinal equipment categories and avoid monetized supplements.
- Test the guidance without revenue first, then add disclosed affiliate links.
- Keep editorial decisions, clinical content, and commercial relationships structurally separate.
